There's something uncomfortable about showing unfinished work. We're trained to present polished results — the final commit, the launched product, the case study with clean screenshots.

But the interesting stuff happens in the middle. The dead ends, the rewrites, the moment you realize your entire approach was wrong.

## Why bother

Building in public isn't about self-promotion. It's about **compressing feedback loops**. When you share early, you learn faster. You find the blind spots before they become expensive.

It also forces clarity. If you can't explain what you're building and why, you probably don't understand it well enough yet.

## What I've learned

A few things that surprised me:

- **Nobody cares about your failures** as much as you think they do. People are generous with unfinished work.
- **Writing about your process** helps you think. It's not documentation — it's a thinking tool.
- **Consistency beats quality.** A rough weekly update teaches you more than a polished quarterly post.

The hardest part isn't the vulnerability. It's the discipline to keep showing up when nobody's watching yet.
